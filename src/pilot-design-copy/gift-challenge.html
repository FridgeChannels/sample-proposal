02 · A New Growth Unit

The Ambient Connected Household

A household qualifies as an Ambient Connected Household when its connection to a brand meets all four pillars:

01

Ambient presence.
Seen in daily routines (10+ times/day)—without demanding attention.

02

Off-screen exposure.
A place in the home, not another message on the phone.

03

Direct access.
One tap to a brand-selected shop or reorder page. No search. No detours.

04

Personalized experiences.
Tailor every post-tap experience to each household’s in-home commerce habits—and adapt it to support the brand’s strategic goals.

03 · Measuring the Growth Unit

# The Ambient Connected Household Rate

What it measures

The percentage of a brand’s purchasing households that qualify as Ambient Connected Households by meeting all four pillars.

What it represents

It shows how much of the brand’s customer base has an ongoing, direct, personalized connection—and signals potential for retention, repeat orders, and profitable growth.

08 · Offline Retail: How It Works

From Retail Purchase to an Ambient Connected Household

Increase the Ambient Connected Household Rate among households that buy from offline retailers by turning retail purchases into ongoing in-home brand connections. The post-tap experience supports the brand’s strategic goals while keeping the destination aligned with the retailer through which the product was sold.

Customer Segments & Box Placement

SegmentWhere to insert FCPost-tap focus
Walmart customersProduct packages destined for WalmartTap back to Walmart with a personalized experience aligned with the retailer.
Other retail customersProduct packages destined for the originating retailerTap back to the retailer through which the product was sold, with retailer-matched content and offers.

How It Works

  1. Match FC to the retailer before distribution. Assign each FC touchpoint or batch to a retailer and insert it into product packages destined for that retailer—not shipping boxes. Keep this match intact through distribution.
  2. Bring the connection home. Customers buy the product in-store and place FC on the refrigerator, creating ambient presence and off-screen exposure.
  3. Tap back to the originating retailer. FC supplied with a product sold through Walmart links back to Walmart. FC supplied through another retailer links back to that retailer.
  4. Personalize within the retailer context. Tailor post-tap content to the household’s in-home commerce habits and the brand’s strategic goals. Keep the destination retailer-matched as content and offers change.

10 · Next Steps

Brand: Choose a Channel, Set a Goal, and Agree on Budget

  1. 01

    Select a channel.

  2. 02

    Set the Ambient Connected Household Rate goal.

    For the selected channel, establish the current baseline and agree on a target percentage of purchasing households that will meet all four pillars of an Ambient Connected Household. Define how and when the rate will be measured.

  3. 03

    Decide on the budget.

    Agree on a budget range based on the chosen channel, pilot scope, expected touchpoint volume, and applicable product and performance fees.

Detailed Roadmap

FridgeChannel: Build the Detailed Roadmap

01

Kickoff and success criteria.

Confirm the channel (DTC, Amazon, or offline retail), pilot products, markets, timing, budget, and decision-makers. Record the current purchasing-household baseline, define the target Ambient Connected Household Rate, agree on the measurement window, and document how taps, attributed purchases, repeat orders, subscriptions, and cross-sell will be tracked.

02

Map audiences and segments.

Identify eligible households and define segment rules using available order and customer data—for example, first-order customers, repeat buyers, subscribers, or retail-origin buyers. For each segment, specify eligibility, exclusions, offer or content focus, touchpoint quantity, and the event that triggers insertion or assignment. Confirm data access, consent, and privacy requirements before using customer data.

03

Design the touchpoint.

Agree on format, materials, dimensions, brand assets, QR/NFC or other tap method, instructions, and call to action. Create a production-ready design that is visible and easy to use on a refrigerator. Review a digital proof, then approve a physical sample for readability, durability, scan/tap performance, and packaging fit. Finalize artwork and sign off on quantity and unit cost.

04

Build the post-tap experience.

Create the landing destination and tailored journeys for each approved segment. Set the channel-specific destination: the brand’s DTC shop, an eligible brand-curated Amazon Store page, or the retailer that sold the product. Configure product recommendations, subscription or reorder paths, offers, disclosures, analytics, and fallback behavior. Make content mobile-friendly and ensure each experience supports the agreed brand goals.

05

Set up tracking and operations.

Create unique links or codes by channel, segment, campaign, and—where needed—retailer or batch. Configure analytics and attribution, test event capture, and define a reporting cadence and owner. Document who can update destinations, approve content, resolve issues, and pause a campaign; confirm that remote changes will not break the tap experience.

06

Plan production, fulfillment, and retailer matching.

Approve the final production file and purchase order, schedule manufacturing, and inspect the first production run. For DTC, plan insertion into the selected order or subscription-shipment stream. For Amazon, insert at a brand-controlled production or packaging stage, not into Amazon’s shipping box. For offline retail, assign each touchpoint or batch to its destination retailer before distribution, preserve that match through fulfillment, and verify that the product reaches the correct retailer’s supply path.

07

Prepare the launch.

Set insertion dates, eligible order volumes, inventory buffers, shipment or retailer milestones, and escalation contacts. Train fulfillment or packaging teams with written instructions and sample packs. Verify destination links, segment rules, packaging placement, retailer routing, customer support guidance, and launch reporting before releasing inventory.

08

Run quality checks and release in stages.

Test every link and tap method across representative phones; verify that the correct segment and channel reach the correct page; check accessibility, load speed, tracking, offers, and purchase flow. Start with a small batch, reconcile inserted units against eligible orders or retailer allocations, review early data, then expand only after operational and experience checks pass.

09

Measure, learn, and optimize.

Review the Ambient Connected Household Rate alongside tap rate, conversion, repeat purchase, subscription, cross-sell, attributed sales, and cost per outcome. Compare results by segment, channel, retailer, and cohort where data permits. Identify friction, update content or destinations remotely, adjust insertion volumes or segment rules, and record each change. At the agreed review point, decide whether to scale, revise, or stop the pilot and document the next rollout plan.
